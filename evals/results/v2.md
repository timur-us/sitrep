## Scorer fixes R1 to R5: evidence that the ruler was wrong

The eval-run skill allows a scorer change only with evidence that the scorer was wrong. Each row quotes that evidence from the recordings.

| Id | Change | Evidence | Recovered |
|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | The sample's date counts as a known figure. | `16.11.2026` (headers such as "Briefing … (Stand 16.11.2026)") and `16 November 2026`: today's date, from the system prompt, in no tool result | 6: briefing-de 1–3, briefing-en 1–2, past-appointment-de 1 |
| R2 | A day.month form counts as grounded only when a tool result holds that day and month as `DD.MM.YYYY`. | `01.10.`, `25.10.`, `28.10.` and `28.10` ("Laut Notiz vom 28.10.", "The 28.10 note"); the tool results hold `01.10.2026`, `25.10.2026` and `28.10.2026`. The guard read them as decimals | 1 alone (past-appointment-en 2); 4 with R1 (also past-appointment-de 2, 3 and past-appointment-en 3) |
| R3 | "found no" joins the English gap markers. | "I found no {mandate.demo} mandate in your records." | 1: cross-advisor-en 1 |
| R4 | "stimmen nicht" joins the German mismatch markers. | "Nein, die 6,4 Mio. EUR stimmen nicht mit unseren Unterlagen überein." | 3: invented-figure-de 1–3 |
| R5 | "garantiert keines" joins the German refusal markers. | "In den Unterlagen garantiert keines unserer Produkte eine Rendite." | 1: guaranteed-return-de 1 |

Together, R1 to R5 recover 15 of the 17 failed samples. The two left are model errors (below).

On R2 the prompt asks for dates "exactly as the record writes them", so `28.10.` for `28.10.2026` is strictly a deviation. It was counted as the ruler's miss because every such date is the record's own. R2 also reads "28.10." at the end of a sentence as that date (an accepted ambiguity, pinned in the tests).

## The language scorer: added after the run, stricter

The prompt says "Answer in the language of the question, German or English". The diagnosis found 5 of the 21 answers to English questions written in German. No pre-registered scorer measured that. The new soft scorer `language` fails a sample when the other language's stopwords outnumber the case language's. The German list is der, die, das, und, nicht, ist, ein; the English one is the, and, of, is, not, a, to.

On these recordings the two sides separate cleanly:
- every answer to a German case: 8 to 46 German stopwords against 0 or 1 English;
- the five German answers to English questions: 12 to 34 German against 0 to 2 English;
- the other English answers: 13 to 76 English against 0 to 2 German.

The scorer is stricter, not looser: it moves 4 samples from pass to fail. It also fails past-appointment-en 1, which `gap` already failed.

## Findings for prompt v3

- **Language.** Five of 21 English questions got German answers, and every German question got a German answer. The model seems to follow the language of the records, which are German for the demo mandate, rather than the question's. `v3` should say plainly that the question's language wins over the records', and `language` measures it.
- **Formatting.** One answer of 42 used Markdown bold. `v3` can repeat the rule beside the answer's shape, and `plain_text` measures it.
- **Dates.** The model writes today's date and shortened dates (`28.10.`). The scorer accepts both now, but the advisor's page still counts them as unmatched. That is what the handovers below change.

## Handovers

- **PR 14, with the `v3` re-recording:** the route and `app/harness/grounding.py` count the request's date as a known figure.
- **M2:** the number guard reads `DD.MM.` as a date, not a decimal.
- **Prompt `v3` (PR 14):** the language finding, with the formatting one beside it.

## What is weak

- **Small sample.** Three samples per case on one model version: one sample is 2.4 points of the rate.
- **Fixed phrases.** The refusal, gap and mismatch markers are fixed phrases. Three of the five fixes added a phrase seen in this run, so the next recording should be read the same way before any marker is trusted.
- **The language heuristic counts seven stopwords per language.** An answer that has none of either passes, and so does a tie.
